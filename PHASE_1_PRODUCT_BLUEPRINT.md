## 0. Business and current-state context

### Business

ItsMyToy is a small children’s-toy business that sells directly to retail customers and also supplies products to other shopkeepers. The business is currently operated by its two owners, Anmol and Muskan. When the owners are away from the physical shop, a family member or an employee may handle customer enquiries and sales.

The shop contains products organised by SKU, category, variant and physical rack location. Individual products have a purchase cost, a standard selling price and an MRP. Customers frequently ask for an additional discount, but the person serving the customer may not know the lowest permitted price.

### Current system

The business does not currently use any POS, ERP, inventory or accounting application.

The only operational record is a manually maintained spreadsheet workbook named **ItsMyToy - Inventory & Accounting**, currently shared through Google Sheets. The workbook contains these main sheets:

- **Inventory Master:** product, price and calculated stock information;
- **Sales Log:** one row per sold product line;
- **Customers:** customer contact information and calculated purchase history;
- **Operations:** calculated business indicators and data-quality exceptions.

Because people can type into formula and formatting cells, the workbook is vulnerable to accidental overwrites, inconsistent terminology and incomplete sales records. The new application will import relevant workbook records, report ambiguous or invalid rows for review and then become the primary operational source of truth. The workbook will remain a read-only historical reference after a successful migration.

### Defined user roles

- **Business owner:** a person with full operational authority. The initial owner accounts will belong to Anmol and Muskan.
- **Store operator:** any employee or family member who serves customers and records retail sales.
- **Trusted store operator:** a store operator who has been given additional discount or stock-receiving authority.
- **Stock receiver:** an authorised user who records products arriving from a supplier. An owner may also perform this role.
- **Customer:** a retail buyer. A customer record is optional for an ordinary guest sale unless a business rule makes it mandatory.
- **Wholesale customer:** another business or shopkeeper buying products for resale. Full wholesale ordering is outside Phase 1.

### Terminology

- **SKU:** the business’s unique identifier for a specific product or product variant.
- **Barcode:** the machine-readable value scanned by a phone camera or barcode scanner. A barcode resolves to one SKU.
- **Product variant:** a distinct colour, size or model that requires its own stock quantity.
- **Purchase cost:** the amount paid by ItsMyToy for one sellable unit. This was labelled `PP` in the workbook.
- **MRP:** the maximum retail price displayed to the customer.
- **Standard selling price:** the normal planned selling price before an additional shop-floor discount. This was labelled `SP` or `Standard S.Price` in the workbook.
- **Final unit price:** the actual amount charged for one unit after any permitted discount.
- **Price floor:** the minimum final unit price a particular user role may approve without asking a higher-authority user.
- **Customer savings:** MRP minus the final unit price. This is the customer-facing discount.
- **Internal markdown:** standard selling price minus the final unit price. This is an internal business measure.
- **Line total:** quantity multiplied by final unit price. It is generated by the application and is never entered manually.
- **Sellable stock:** units currently available for sale, excluding damaged or supplier-return stock.
- **Stock movement:** an immutable record that increases or decreases a SKU’s stock for a stated reason.
- **Stock receipt:** the record created when products are received from a supplier. It is an operational receiving record, not an accounting bill.
- **Supplier bill/invoice reference:** the number or text printed on the supplier’s source document and stored on a stock receipt to support later verification.
- **Payment mode:** how the customer paid, such as cash, UPI or card.
- **Sales channel:** where the sale originated, such as a physical-store walk-in, WhatsApp enquiry or wholesale enquiry.

## 1. Product outcome

Any authorised store operator should be able to receive or sell a toy correctly without calling a business owner for routine decisions, while the system keeps stock, pricing authority, payment details and accountability under control.

Phase 1 is a daily shop-operations product. It will replace the workbook for new day-to-day operational entries after migration. Statutory invoicing, tax compliance and full accounting are separate later requirements.

### Success looks like this

- A store operator can scan and complete a normal retail sale in under 45 seconds.
- Most retail sales do not require a phone call to an owner.
- No user can sell below their permitted price without approval.
- Every completed sale reduces stock exactly once.
- Stock cannot be overwritten directly; every change has a reason and history.
- New stock can be received and checked against a supplier bill.
- Business owners can see sales, stock and exceptions remotely.
- Poor connectivity is visible and does not silently lose or duplicate a sale.

## 3. Users and authority

| Role | Primary job | Discount authority | Cost/profit visibility | Stock authority |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Store operator | Scan and complete retail sales | Store-operator price floor | Hidden | View only |
| Trusted store operator | Retail sales plus explicitly granted responsibilities | Trusted-operator price floor | Hidden | May receive existing items and request corrections |
| Business owner | Full operational control | Owner price floor and exceptional approval | Full | Full, with recorded reasons |
| Accountant, later | Reconciliation and accounting | None | Financial reports | No operational edits |

### Role rules

- Only an owner can create a new product or change a SKU/barcode.
- A trusted store operator can receive stock for an existing product only when the permission is explicitly enabled.
- A store operator cannot view purchase cost, gross profit or owner-only price limits.
- No one, including an owner, directly edits completed sales or the stock balance.
- Owners correct mistakes through cancellation, return or stock-adjustment movements.

## 4. Core journey A: a store operator handles a discounted retail sale

### Happy path

1. User opens **Sell**.
2. User scans the product barcode/SKU.
3. App shows:
   - product photo and name;
   - variant;
   - last-synced available quantity;
   - rack location;
   - MRP;
   - standard selling price;
   - lowest price this user may give.
4. Standard selling price is selected by default.
5. User can choose:
   - Standard Price;
   - Extra 5% Off the standard selling price;
   - Extra 10% Off the standard selling price, when permitted;
   - Round Price, where the user enters a target final unit price that must remain within the permitted range;
   - a bounded price slider whose upper value is the standard selling price and lower value is the current user's price floor.
6. App calculates final unit price, customer savings and cart total automatically.
7. User adds the item to the cart and scans more products if needed.
8. At checkout, user selects Guest or finds/adds a customer.
9. User selects payment mode and sales channel.
10. User reviews the final cart and taps **Complete Sale**.
11. The system creates the sale, sale lines, payment and outward stock movements as one transaction.
12. A success screen offers receipt sharing and New Sale.

### Price display contract

- **Starting price:** standard selling price.
- **Customer savings:** MRP minus final price.
- **Internal markdown:** standard selling price minus final price.
- **Profit:** final price minus the purchase-cost snapshot.
- A store operator sees the starting price, final price and customer savings, but not purchase cost or profit.
- Owners can see all four.
- Quantity, unit price and line total are always separate fields.
- Line total is always calculated as quantity multiplied by unit price; it is never manually entered.

### Approval path

If the chosen price falls below the user's floor:

1. Add to Cart becomes **Request Approval**.
2. User enters a short reason, such as regular customer, damaged box or bulk quantity.
3. All active business-owner accounts receive an in-app approval request.
4. An owner approves, rejects or replaces the price.
5. Approval is attached to that sale line and cannot be reused for another sale.

If connectivity is unavailable, below-floor sales are blocked. An optional owner PIN override can be evaluated after the first release.

### Sale exceptions

- **Barcode cannot scan:** manual SKU/name search.
- **Unknown barcode:** owner can link it to an existing product or create a product.
- **Stock is zero:** block normal completion; owner can investigate or override with a recorded reason.
- **Only one unit remains and device is offline:** require connection or owner override.
- **Customer changes their mind:** remove from cart before completion.
- **Mistake after completion:** cancel/return workflow; never edit or delete the sale.
- **Split payment:** deferred unless it is confirmed as a frequent real requirement.

## 5. Core journey B: an authorised stock receiver records incoming stock

### Existing product

1. Open **Stock → Receive Stock**.
2. Select or add the supplier and enter bill/invoice reference.
3. Scan the first product.
4. App shows current product, stock, last purchase cost, MRP, selling price and rack.
5. Enter received sellable quantity.
6. Enter damaged/missing quantity separately when applicable.
7. Confirm purchase cost, MRP and standard selling price.
8. Confirm or change rack location.
9. Add more receipt lines.
10. App shows expected bill quantity/value versus entered quantity/value.
11. Save as Draft or tap **Complete Receipt**.
12. Completion creates inward stock movements and price/cost history.

### New product

Only an owner can create it. Minimum required fields:

- Product name
- SKU, generated and checked for uniqueness
- Variant when applicable
- Barcode; more than one barcode may be linked later
- Category and sub-category
- Brand, optional
- Purchase cost
- Standard selling price
- MRP
- Salesperson, manager and owner price floors
- Opening/received quantity
- Unit or pack
- Pack size when relevant
- Rack location
- Product photo, recommended

### Receipt safety rules

- A receipt remains editable while Draft.
- A completed receipt is locked.
- Corrections create an adjustment or supplier-return movement.
- Changing current purchase cost does not change historical sale profit.
- Damaged stock never increases sellable stock.
- The same supplier invoice reference triggers a possible-duplicate warning.
- Large price or quantity changes trigger a review warning, not silent acceptance.

## 8. Inventory truth model

The application must not store stock as a manually editable number.

### Stock movement types

- Opening balance
- Purchase receipt
- Retail sale
- Sale cancellation
- Customer return
- Supplier return
- Damage
- Lost/missing
- Sample/personal use
- Stock-count adjustment
- Reservation and transfer, later

### Stock states

- Sellable
- Damaged
- Reserved, later
- Return to supplier

### Calculation

`On hand = total inward movements - total outward movements`

For each SKU, the owner can open a timeline that explains the complete balance. A stock movement has a signed quantity, reason, reference record, user and timestamp.

## 10. Offline and sync contract

The PWA should cache the active product catalogue, barcodes, permitted prices and last-known stock on each authorised device.

### When online

- Scans and writes use the central database.
- Stock is checked immediately before sale completion.
- Owner approvals are real-time.

### When offline

- Product search and permitted pricing continue.
- Completed normal-price sales enter a visible **Queued for Sync** state.
- The device deducts its own queued quantities from the displayed cached stock.
- Below-floor pricing and unknown-product creation are blocked.
- Selling the last known unit requires connection or owner override.
- The app always displays Offline / Last synced time.

### On reconnect

- Every queued action carries a unique device transaction key.
- Retrying cannot create a duplicate sale or stock movement.
- Conflicts appear in the owner's exception queue.
- The device must not show Synced until the server confirms the transaction.

## 11. Mistake-prevention rules

1. Generated values are read-only: on-hand stock, gross sales, line total, savings and profit.
2. SKU and barcode uniqueness is checked before save.
3. Dropdowns and controlled choices replace free text where practical.
4. Dangerous actions require both a reason and appropriate authority.
5. Completed business records are immutable.
6. No hard delete for products, sales, receipts or movements.
7. Price and cost snapshots preserve historical truth.
8. Every stock movement has a source or explicit adjustment reason.
9. The app warns before zero-price, below-cost or zero/negative-stock actions.
10. Save Draft is automatic during carts and receipts.
11. Sync status is prominent, not hidden in settings.
12. Owner views default to unresolved exceptions.

## 12. Migration from the ItsMyToy - Inventory & Accounting workbook

### Importable product fields

- SKU
- Item name
- Category and sub-category
- Entry type/variant information
- Brand
- Purchase price
- Standard selling price
- MRP
- On-hand quantity, as an opening-balance movement
- Notes and rack/location when available

### Importable sales fields

- Date and sale ID
- Customer and phone
- SKU and item
- Quantity
- MRP, purchase-price and standard-price snapshots
- Actual unit selling price
- Payment mode and channel
- Notes

Gross sales, discounts and profit will be recalculated during migration rather than trusted as independent inputs.

### Importable customer fields

- Customer ID, name and phone
- WhatsApp/email
- Child details
- Address/locality and source

First visit, last visit, purchase lines and total spend will be derived from imported sales.

### Pre-import exception report

The importer must stop or quarantine:

- Duplicate SKUs or barcodes
- Products missing cost, selling price or MRP
- Sales with zero actual selling price
- Sales where unit price versus line total is ambiguous
- Sales referencing a missing SKU
- Invalid or duplicate customer phone records
- Negative or unexplained opening stock
- Missing payment mode or channel

After the migration is accepted, the workbook remains read-only historical evidence. It does not remain a second editable source of operational truth.

## 14. Phase 1 acceptance checks

### Selling

- Scanning a known barcode opens the correct SKU/variant.
- A permitted price can be completed without owner involvement.
- A below-floor price cannot be completed without approval.
- A completed quantity of 2 creates one sale line and an outward movement of 2.
- Retrying after a network interruption does not create a duplicate sale.
- Cost and profit are hidden from a salesperson.

### Stock receiving

- Completing a receipt increases only the received SKU quantities.
- Damaged quantity does not increase sellable quantity.
- A completed receipt cannot be edited.
- An adjustment requires a reason and appropriate approval.
- Product history exactly reconciles to the displayed on-hand balance.

### Owner control

- The owner sees a new sale and discount request remotely.
- The owner can explain a SKU balance through its movement history.
- Unsynced sales are visible by device and user.
- Zero-price or missing-critical-field records cannot silently enter reports.

### Migration

- Every imported product produces one controlled product/variant record.
- Imported opening stock produces opening-balance movements.
- Historical sale totals are generated from quantity and actual unit price.
- Rejected or ambiguous rows appear in a downloadable exception report.

## 15. Accepted constraints to validate during screen design

The owners accepted the governing rules in `BUSINESS_DECISIONS.md` on 21 July 2026. Screen design must make those rules easy to follow without weakening them. Real shop examples should validate:

1. each person can understand the capability allowed by their assigned role;
2. the ₹5,000 customer-information prompt does not block a permitted Guest override;
3. split payments are entered as separate Cash, UPI, Card or Bank Transfer amounts;
4. discount controls clearly stop at the signed-in role’s accepted floor;
5. a trusted operator can prepare, but not complete, a stock receipt;
6. the one-unit offline safety reserve and 12-hour grace are clearly communicated;
7. owner-approved return, cancellation, below-cost and open-box flows cannot be mistaken for ordinary sales;
8. the internal SKU barcode and supplier alternate barcode resolve to one product variant;
9. rack codes use the accepted `L1-S1` style with S1 at the bottom and S6 at the top.

Usability findings may improve wording, layout and guidance. A finding that changes authority, pricing, stock or retention follows formal decision change control.

1. build the accepted production walking skeleton;
2. verify deployment, backend authorization, PostgreSQL transactions, performance, cost and recovery;
3. begin business-feature slices only after that gate passes.

A mobile-first clickable prototype remains optional. If created, it validates usability for the Store-operator Home, Product scan/pricing, Cart/checkout, Receive stock and Owner approval/dashboard screens.
